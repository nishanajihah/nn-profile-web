# Legal Terms Writing Guidelines

This document serves as a cheat sheet and checklist for writing legal terms for future websites, landing pages, and applications. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The terminology you use and the clauses you include should directly reflect the *type* of project you are building. Always customize the terms to match what your application actually does.

## 1. Terminology: Which one should I use?

Although they are all legally binding contracts that accomplish the exact same thing, industry standards dictate which term feels most appropriate for your specific project:

* **Terms of Use (ToU):** 
  * *Best for:* Informational websites, portfolios, blogs, or static landing pages.
  * *Why:* Users are simply "using" or browsing the site to read content. They aren't logging in or buying anything.
* **Terms of Service (ToS):**
  * *Best for:* Web apps, mobile apps, SaaS (Software as a Service), APIs, and games.
  * *Why:* You are providing a digital "service" that users interact with, log into, or rely on.
* **Terms & Conditions (T&C):**
  * *Best for:* E-commerce stores, retail, subscription sites, or general corporate sites.
  * *Why:* It implies the "conditions" of a transaction, purchase, or formal agreement. It is the most universally recognized term.

*Note: When in doubt, **Terms & Conditions** is universally accepted everywhere.*

## 2. Core Requirements Checklist

When drafting new terms for a project, ensure you include these essential sections:

- [ ] **Agreement to Terms:** A clear statement that by accessing the site/app, the user agrees to be bound by the terms.
- [ ] **Intellectual Property (IP):** State who owns the code, designs, music, graphics, and content. Prohibit unauthorized commercial reproduction.
- [ ] **Third-Party Attribution:** If you use third-party APIs (like Spotify, GitHub, Stripe, OpenAI) or open-source assets that require attribution, explicitly state that their respective owners retain those rights.
- [ ] **User Obligations / Acceptable Use:** Set rules for how users can behave. (e.g., "Do not reverse engineer the app", "Do not spam the API", "Do not upload malicious files"). *Crucial for apps with user accounts.*
- [ ] **Disclaimers:** The "As Is" clause. State that the software/service is provided as-is without warranties. This protects you if there are bugs or downtime.
- [ ] **Limitation of Liability:** A critical clause stating you are not legally responsible for financial damages, data loss, or business interruptions caused by the user using your app.
- [ ] **Governing Law:** Explicitly state the jurisdiction (e.g., **Malaysia**) where any legal disputes would be resolved. This automatically sets the baseline for international interactions.
- [ ] **Modifications:** State that you reserve the right to update or change the terms at any time.
- [ ] **Contact Information:** Provide an email address for legal inquiries.

## 3. Formatting Best Practices

> [!TIP]
> Keep the document structured. Users usually skim legal text, so clear headers protect you by making the rules obvious.

1. **Use Numbered Sections:** (e.g., 1. Disclaimer, 2. Governing Law) to make it easy to reference specific rules.
2. **Bold Key Terms:** Use bold text for important concepts like **Malaysia**, **Intellectual Property**, or **"AS IS"** to draw attention.
3. **External Links:** Always include a link back to your Privacy Policy within the terms to show they are connected.
4. **Last Updated Date:** Always put the effective date at the top of the page.
